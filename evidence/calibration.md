# Calibration — mean confidence (correct vs wrong) and Brier score

_Generated by `python3 scripts/eval_calibration.py` from committed evidence tables; no live runs. No logic changes between runs — presentation only._

**Brier score (one-vs-rest on the chosen verdict).** For every row with predicted verdict V at confidence p, the target probability is 1 if the actual verdict was V (row ok) and 0 otherwise. The per-row loss is `(p - 1)^2` when ok and `p^2` when wrong, averaged across rows. Lower is better; range [0, 1]; a confident wrong answer costs the most.

Columns:
- **ok/n** — correct verdicts over counted rows (same rows as the evidence tables).
- **mean conf · correct** — mean confidence on rows the system got right.
- **mean conf · wrong** — mean confidence on rows the system got wrong.
- **Brier** — one-vs-rest Brier score across all rows.

## Honest reading

- **A0 is better-calibrated than B0 and M0 on dev** (Brier 0.0905 vs 0.295 vs 0.2572). On the blind hb set the three sit close together (0.21–0.24): B0 and M0 keep confidence high on both correct and wrong rows, A0 trims confidence when a case escalates to a human.
- **M0 is the worst-calibrated across every set.** Its mean confidence on wrong rows is 0.80+ in every column — Nemotron alone is confident even when it is wrong. That is the symptom the agent architecture is built to cushion.
- **A0 keeps a visible gap between correct-confidence and wrong-confidence on dev + ho.** On hb the gap narrows (0.729 vs 0.654) because the three hb misses are all mid-confidence over-escalations the agent surfaces to a human rather than committing to a verdict — the policy adds a -0.1 degraded humility penalty on contested chains.
- **Zero false contain / false benign on A0 across all three sets is still the headline.** Calibration adds colour; the error-shape invariant (never silently lock a real user, never silently clear a real attack) is what makes the Brier number meaningful.
